AI, energy, water, materials and infrastructure · Updated 19 July 2026

AI has a footprint.
A prompt does not have one number.

Short text, long reasoning, image and video generation can demand very different amounts of computation. Water figures change with the boundary. Efficient individual requests can still add up to rising infrastructure demand. This explainer keeps those labels attached.

01 · Read the label first

Six questions come before the number

The word prompt does not define an environmental footprint. Every number belongs to a particular task, system and accounting method. Change any of them and the result may change too.

What task was performed?

A short text reply, long-context synthesis, deliberate reasoning, an image, a video and an agent using several tools are different tasks. They cannot be compared without further context.

Production measureA named service and date; closest to use in the wild.
Controlled benchmarkBetter for comparisons; not a whole public service.
Modelled estimateUseful when meters are absent; assumptions drive the result.
Provider LCABroader lifecycle view; provider authorship remains visible.
Sector outlookInfrastructure scale and scenarios; not one prompt.

Reading rule: if the task, system, boundary, location, date or measurement method is missing, the number is only a point of reference—not the footprint of your request.1234

The in-force ITU-T L.1801 recommendation follows the same logic: define the functional unit, system boundary and lifecycle stages before selecting relevant impacts such as climate, water, resources and land.17

02 · One measured text prompt

0.24 Wh is a strong data point—and a narrow one

Google instrumented its production serving stack and reported the median Gemini Apps text prompt in May 2025. The study counted accelerator power, host CPU and memory, provisioned idle capacity and data-centre overhead.2

Do not relabel this as “the cost of an AI prompt”. The value describes one provider, one product class and one month. The authors work for Google, and the paper is a preprint. This is valuable production evidence, not an independent universal benchmark.

03 · Task choice

Reasoning can change the energy more than the word “prompt” suggests

ML.ENERGY compared 46 models across seven tasks and 1,858 H100/B200 configurations. In one controlled same-model comparison, Qwen 3 32B used much more energy for problem solving than for ordinary conversation.3

04 · Water

Three published figures. Three different questions.

Disagreement about “water per prompt” often begins with studies measuring different systems, workloads and parts of the lifecycle. The three familiar figures belong together only when those differences remain visible.

Scope 1 · On siteWater consumed at the data centre, often through evaporative cooling.
Scope 2 · ElectricityWater used off site to generate the electricity serving the facility.
Scope 3 · Supply chainWater associated with manufacturing chips, servers and other equipment.

The three-scope description follows Li et al.; water withdrawal and water consumption are related but different measures.4

05 · From a request to the system

Small per-use numbers can coexist with fast infrastructure growth

The IEA's April 2026 central outlook puts electricity use by all data centres at 485 TWh in 2025 and 950 TWh in 2030—roughly double, or around 3% of global electricity demand by 2030. It separately projects AI-focused data-centre consumption to triple.1

Efficiency paradox

The IEA reports that energy per AI task has fallen by at least an order of magnitude annually in recent years. Yet uptake and more intensive use cases pushed total data-centre demand upward.1

Efficiency changes the unit cost; it does not decide how many units society uses.

UN research reportLocation and distribution

A low-carbon option is not automatically low-water or low-land

UNU-INWEH treats location as an environmental and distributional question. Electricity demand, water withdrawals, land use, mineral extraction and e-waste can be concentrated around particular communities even when the benefits of AI are distributed elsewhere.19

Carbon, water and land footprints can move in different directions. Siting decisions therefore require several indicators, not a carbon score alone.

06 · What can—and cannot—be compared

AI is one part of a wider digital footprint

Video and music streaming, social media, web browsing and video calls also depend on devices, networks, data centres and physical hardware. Putting AI in this wider picture is useful, but only if each number keeps its own service, date, unit and system boundary.

Production measureGemini Apps · May 2025
0.24 Whper median text prompt

A provider-authored full-stack measurement for one named service and workload. It is operational energy, not a lifecycle footprint for AI in general.2

Historical IEA estimateVideo streaming · 2019
≈77 Whone hour of typical viewing

The IEA's updated 2019 scenario includes the viewing device, data transmission and data centre. The result changes substantially with the screen, connection and resolution; this is a historical orientation, not a current streaming constant.22

Peer-reviewed LCA modelAnnual digital-content profile
229 kg CO₂eone modelled year of use

A 2024 study modelled 3,230 hours of web use, social media, video and music streaming and video calls for a global-average user archetype. It covers devices, networks and data centres from raw-material extraction to end of life; behaviour data came from 2019–2022 and technology data mostly from 2020.21

These cards do not form a single scale. One is electricity for one request, one is electricity for an hour, and one is lifecycle greenhouse-gas emissions for a modelled year. No defensible “one activity equals so many of the other” ratio follows from them.

What the comparison does tell us

Digital impacts are not confined to generative AI, and the user's device can matter as much as remote infrastructure. At the same time, a low value for one narrow text request does not describe intensive AI tasks or system-wide growth. A meaningful assessment first defines its functional unit and system boundary—two requirements made explicit in ITU-T L.1801.17

The earlier “8.6 seconds of a 100 W television” example is only a conversion of 0.24 Wh against a fixed 100 W load. It is not a comparison with an hour of streamed television or with the television's lifecycle footprint.

07 · Training and hardware

The footprint does not end when inference stops

Training, server manufacture, replacement and disposal sit outside a narrow per-request electricity figure. Some providers publish parts of this picture; coverage and accounting are uneven.

Provider model card

Meta · Llama 3.1 family

Meta reports 39.3 million H100 GPU-hours and 11,390 tCO₂e of location-based training emissions. The same card reports zero market-based training emissions after renewable-energy matching.10

Zero market-based is an accounting result, not zero physical emissions. Keeping both views visible is more informative than choosing the friendlier one.

Provider lifecycle study

Mistral · LCA disclosure

Mistral published greenhouse-gas, water and resource-depletion indicators for Large 2 and Le Chat. It also calls the study a first approximation and says embodied GPU factors had to be approximated.5

The disclosure is useful because it states its limits. It is not an independently measured league table.

Development and material demand are easy to miss

Measured development programmeOne model series

Development is more than the final training run

An ICLR 2025 study followed a series of language models from 20 million to 13 billion active parameters. With hardware manufacture, development and final training included, the programme had a footprint of at least 493 tCO₂e and used at least 2.769 million litres of water. Development activity amounted to about half the impact of final training.16

This is one development programme and data centre—not a universal multiplier for every model.

Physical material analysisOne Nvidia A100

Specialised hardware is a material system

A 2026 study chemically characterised an Nvidia A100 accelerator and detected 32 elements. It then linked computational demand to the share of hardware lifetime consumed, making equipment lifespan part of the environmental question.18

The analysis covers the GPU only. Networking, storage, racks, power delivery and cooling are excluded, so it is not a full data-centre material footprint.

08 · What EU rules actually disclose

Reporting is improving, but it is not yet a public per-prompt label

Binding EU act
≥500 kW

Data-centre reporting threshold

Regulation (EU) 2024/1364 covers data centres with at least 500 kW of installed IT power demand. Indicators include energy, power utilisation, waste heat, water and renewable energy.11

The European database is public at aggregated level; protected business information and submitted KPIs remain confidential.

AI Act · Annex XI
Compute + energy

GPAI technical documentation

Annex XI lists computational resources, training time and known or estimated model energy. If energy is unknown, it may be derived from computational-resource information.12

That is model technical documentation—not a guarantee that every user gets a public, product-level footprint for each request.

European Commission roadmapForthcoming—not yet an operative label
2026 → 2027

A broader data-centre rating scheme is planned

The Commission's June 2026 roadmap schedules a rating scheme covering energy efficiency, water efficiency, clean energy, waste-heat reuse and flexibility for adoption in 2026, with the first labels planned for 2027.20

The roadmap records the policy timetable. It is not itself the delegated rating act and should not be described as a label already available to users.

09 · Czech context

Local infrastructure is not evidence of request routing

Public-institution announcementProject announcement—not evidence of request routing

IT4Innovations announced the official launch of the Czech AI Factory project on 12 May 2026. The announcement gives a budget of nearly CZK 1 billion and says approximately half will support acquisition and operation of the KarolAIna supercomputer.13

The announcement confirms a Czech public-infrastructure project. It does not show where an ordinary commercial chatbot processed a request. A regional cloud list or national AI project cannot replace service-specific routing evidence.

10 · Decisions that survive uncertainty

Reduce avoidable demand; demand better evidence

For an individual task

  • Use the least intensive mode that can do the job.
  • Reserve extended reasoning, video and multi-step agents for cases that need their added capability.
  • Keep the input context and requested output only as long as the task requires.
  • Reuse a good result instead of regenerating it without a purpose.

These choices follow the measured task and output-length mechanisms; they do not promise a fixed percentage saving.13

For an institution

  • Count use by task class and output, not only by “prompts”.
  • Keep location-based and market-based emissions separate.
  • Ask for water withdrawal and consumption across relevant scopes.
  • Include hardware lifetime, reuse and end-of-life plans in procurement.

An honest, transparent answer may still contain uncertainty. Without these labels, comparisons are unreliable.

11 · Myth check

Can the claim survive with its labels attached?

“An AI prompt uses 0.24 Wh.”
Missing scope. The value belongs to the May 2025 median Gemini Apps text prompt under Google's defined full-stack method. It is not a universal prompt constant.
“AI used 485 TWh of electricity in 2025.”
Wrong category. The IEA's 485 TWh value covers all data centres. AI-focused data centres are a subset.
“No provider has ever published training compute or emissions.”
False absolute. Meta's Llama 3.1 model card reports GPU-hours and both location- and market-based emissions. The defensible criticism is that disclosure remains uneven and non-standardised.
“Mistral's 45 mL means its response is about 173 times thirstier than Google's 0.26 mL.”
Invalid comparison. The services, workloads, methods, dates and water boundaries differ. Arithmetic cannot repair unlike denominators.
“If each task gets more efficient, total electricity demand must fall.”
Scale breaks the inference. The IEA reports rapid per-task efficiency gains alongside 17% growth in total data-centre electricity demand during 2025.

Conclusion

The honest answer is conditional, not evasive

AI use has environmental impacts, and “one prompt” is not a defensible unit for measuring them. A short text request can have a small impact within a specific production measurement. Reasoning, video and agentic work can be far more intensive. At system scale, the pace of adoption and infrastructure growth also matters.

The practical response is neither guilt over every useful query nor indifference to scale. Use the capability proportionately, avoid needless high-compute work, and require providers and institutions to report task, system, boundary, location, date and method.

The system boundary must also reach beyond the server room. Hardware manufacture, land use, water stress, electricity supply and equipment disposal all occur in specific places. The communities carrying these burdens may not be those receiving most of the benefit.161819

Sources and methodology

Where the numbers come from

The page draws on production measurements, controlled benchmarks, modelling studies, lifecycle assessments, official statistics and EU legislation. Company studies are used only for claims about their own systems, and their origin is made explicit. Preprints and forecasts are clearly labelled. This is a source-grounded explainer, not a systematic review or an independent lifecycle assessment.

Methods and limitations

Every quantitative, regulatory and synthesis claim was checked in the cited source. Full texts were available for the added work on water, model development, assessment methods, materials, e-waste, wider digital use and the UNU-INWEH report.

Different methods, boundaries and time horizons are stated beside the relevant values. Measurements, modelled estimates, provider studies and forecasts are therefore not treated as interchangeable. The full UNU-INWEH report supports the qualitative statement about land and unequal distribution of impacts, but its sector-level synthesis and examples do not establish the same effect for every host community.
